# Science Intelligence™ Deck — Structure & Slide Archetypes

Deliver as an unbranded, content-first draft on 16:9 slides — **Claude Design applies branding and exports the final file.** `brand-system.md` is intent guidance for Design, not something to render here. One idea per slide, lots of `#f0f0f0` breathing room. Study tables and claim tables are the backbone — they must be clean, aligned, and readable.

Use **current site language** for section names and taglines (Science Intelligence™, Lit Review™, Claim Dev™, "Science leads, creativity amplifies," Prove/Persuade/Produce). Do not import deck-specific coinages from older examples.

## The organizing principle: claim first, evidence second, always together

This deck is presented live in a one-hour meeting. The client is a brand team. They did not come to read a literature review; they came to find out what the science lets them say. The old shape (here is an active, here are its studies, here is a spotlight, repeat, then finally the claims) made them wait forty minutes for the payoff and asked them to hold the evidence in their heads until it mattered. It read as a slog because it was one.

The new shape inverts that:

1. **Payoff in the first three minutes.** The single most ownable finding, and the claim it becomes, is slide 3.
2. **The claim board is the map.** All priority directions on one slide, up front. Everything after is the receipt.
3. **Evidence is organised by claim, not by active.** Each priority direction is a short chapter: the claim, then the studies that hold it up, side by side. No study is ever shown without the line it lets the brand say. A study that does not attach to a claim goes to the record at the back.
4. **Every slide ends in a payoff line.** An evidence slide closes with `So the brand can say: "…" · CLM-nn`. If a slide cannot end that way, it belongs in the appendix.
5. **The deck asks for a decision.** It ends by asking the client to pick their top three directions. That pick is the input to Science Story; the deck is built to get it.

Lit Review™ and Claim Dev™ remain the two named parts. Claim Dev™ is now the spine (Parts I–III below). Lit Review™ is the record (Part IV). Say this on slide 2 so the client knows where the full evidence lives and stops waiting for it.

## Default flow (a starting shape — adapt to what the project has)

Target **22–28 slides in the main body**, plus appendix. Time budget for a 60-minute meeting is in brackets; the speaker note on each slide should fit it.

**Open — three slides, no more** [5 min]

1. **Cover** — logo top-left; eyebrow "SCIENCE MARKETING ENGINE"; product name very large (with ® if applicable); **"SCIENCE INTELLIGENCE™ PRESENTATION"**; 3pt black rule; "Prepared for [Client] · Prepared by Let There Be · [Month Year]". MLR line. Mitosis gradient blobs bleeding off two corners.
2. **What you're about to see** — four numbers in a row: studies read · actives (or pillars) covered · claims the evidence supports · directions we think are yours to own. Underneath, three plain sentences: what we did, how this deck is ordered (claims first, the full record at the back), and what we want from you by the end (your top three). Speaker note: this replaces the old opportunity / model / engagement / contents / legend slides. If the client is new to LTB and needs the engine explained, that is one sentence here and one slide in the close, not five slides at the front.
3. **The headline** — the Board's headline finding, rendered. Left: the finding, stated plainly, with study and design (e.g. "Zinc lozenges shortened colds by roughly two days in the pivotal RCT · S-014"). Right: the claim it becomes, in quotes, with its Claim ID and a `WORKING LANGUAGE · PENDING MLR` badge. Below: the Board's "why ownable" line. This is rank 1's claim; chapter 1 will return to it with the proof. Speaker note: this is the thirty-second version of the whole engagement.

**Part I — The claim board** [5 min]

4. **Part divider — Claim Dev™** — "What the science lets [Brand] say — the priority directions first, the full record behind them."
5. **The claim board** — the Board's priority table from the Claim Set, rendered as rows: `# | Direction (2–4 words) | Lead claim | Held up by (study count + pivotal Study ID) | Status`. Rank, order, and status come from the Claim Set (`Priority rank`, `Deck status`); this slide displays them and never reorders. Status key: `READY NOW` (product-level evidence in hand) · `CLASS-LEVEL` (published for the ingredient class, not this product) · `NEEDS DETAIL` (awaiting client data). The footer line is the Board's field counts: how many further opportunities are in the library at the back, how many were removed because the client already owns them, and how many were withdrawn on regulatory grounds. Speaker note: "This is the map. The next twenty minutes walk through each row. At the end I'll put this slide back up and ask you which three you want."
6. **Where you stand today** *(one slide, keep it)* — the client's approved claim set and live in-market language, grouped by claim family, so every new direction is visibly built on top of it and not duplicating it. Reference the approval doc code if known. If the client has no approved set yet, drop the slide and say so on the board.
6b. **What the category says today** — the Category Language Map from the Claim Set. Three columns: **The commons** (what two or more competitors already claim, with names), **The white space** (what our evidence supports that no one claims), **The category voice** (the words and taglines the shelf shares, quoted). Footer: competitors scanned and capture date range. If the scan did not run, this slide is one line saying so; do not fake it. Speaker note: "Everything on the board sits in the white space or says a commons claim in a way no one else can. Here's the shelf we checked it against." This slide is why the client believes the word *ownable* for the rest of the hour.

**Part II — The chapters: one per priority direction** [25–30 min total; 4–6 min each]

Each direction gets two slides by default, a third when the material earns it. Eyebrow on every slide in the chapter: `DIRECTION 0N · [TERRITORY]`. Chapter footer: `Direction N of K`.

7a. **The claim** — the chapter opener.
   - Slide headline states the direction in one plain sentence.
   - Badge: `WORKING LANGUAGE · PENDING MLR`.
   - **Lead claim** in quotes, large, with its Claim ID.
   - **What the consumer hears** — the measurement converted per copy-craft.md §3, one line, derivation footnoted. This is the translation slide from the old structure, moved to where the claim is.
   - **Sharper version** — the pack / display / end-frame compression, labeled with where it would run, plus **What changed** in one sentence (copy-craft.md §8).
   - One line: "Held up by N studies, including [pivotal author year · S-nnn]."
   - Speaker note: why this direction, in two sentences; then the pause question for the client, e.g. "Does this feel like a place you'd want to be?" Note their reaction; it is data for the pick at the end.

7b. **The proof** — the evidence that holds the claim up, on one slide.
   - **Element table** across the top: `Part of the claim | What substantiates it | Study IDs | Status`. One row per phrase of the lead claim ("Same SPF" · "Not the same coverage" · "That's what CoverGuard™ is for"), each with its evidence in one or two plain sentences and READY NOW / CLASS-LEVEL / NEEDS DETAIL. This is the substantiation map made visible; the client sees every word is held up.
   - **Pivotal study spotlight** below or beside: author, year, venue, design, n; the one result that matters, as a figure or a three-row outcome table (brand vs comparator vs significance). Soft mint fill on this block.
   - **Supporting rows** — the corroborating and mechanistic studies as a compact table, two to four rows: `Yr | Authors | Design | Finding | Key result`. Finding uses the direction dot + label. If there are more than four, the rest are in the appendix and the footer says so.
   - **The honest line** — one sentence naming what this evidence does not show or where the counter-evidence sits and how the wording accommodates it ("No significant difference on X — the claim stays on Y"). Never omit this; it is the credibility of the chapter.
   - **Payoff line** at the bottom: `So [Brand] can say: "[lead claim]" · CLM-nn`.
   - Speaker note: the thirty-second walk through the table; what would strengthen the direction (a full text, a client data point, a head-to-head).

7c. **The edge** *(optional — include only when the workbook supports it)*
   - **The multiplier** — a defensible ratio or comparison with a comparison table and a card carrying the headline figure, arithmetic disclosed, flagged as directional framing pending MLR.
   - Or **the differentiator** — the competitor's claim, verbatim with source and date from the Competitive Intel tab, beside what this evidence lets the brand say. Where the direction is `Contested`, this slide is not optional; the client needs to see the collision and how our line clears it.
   - Or **the HCP line** — where the direction has a professional register (copy-craft.md §4).
   - Payoff line again, in whichever register the slide serves.

**Part III — The rest of the field** [8 min]

8. **Claim library — the full set**, one or two slides. Columns: `ID | Claim, as [Brand] would say it | Evidence | Level (CLASS / CATEGORY / PRODUCT) | Risk`. Grouped by territory with a count per territory. Rows already covered in Part II are marked with their direction number so the client sees the chapters were drawn from here. Speaker note: these are for building the shortlist, not for reading aloud.
9. **Where the evidence stops** — one card per line the evidence will not support, with the reason (null result, class-only, regulatory class, dose gap). The ✕ list. This is a trust-builder and a selling point of the method; do not skip it and do not soften it. Speaker note: "Everything you've seen is sitting on top of this list. We checked."
10. **Evidence, not claim language** — `ID (SE-nn) | Finding, as the evidence states it | Source | What it substantiates`. Burden data, category facts, the brand's own published methods. Each states a finding and stops. Keeping them separate is what stops a category finding being read as a brand claim.

**Part IV — The record (Lit Review™)** [5 min live, then the client has it]

11. **Part divider — Lit Review™** — "[N] studies across [M] actives — read, mapped, and weighed. The full record behind every claim above."
12. **Evidence base at a glance** — hero number (studies reviewed) + direction breakdown (Positive / Mixed / Null counts) + studies-by-active list with mitosis-gradient bars. Footnote the design mix. Legend for the direction dots lives here now.
13. **Evidence matrix** — actives (or pillars) as rows, outcomes as columns, a mark where evidence lands, with the direction number overlaid on the cells that fed a chapter. This is the one slide where the client sees the whole field at once and sees which parts of it became claims.
14. **Formula map** *(if multi-SKU)* — SKUs across the top, actives down the side.
15. **What the literature tells us** — synthesis: one line per active or pillar naming the lane it earns and the direction number(s) it fed. Where an active earned no lane, say so.

Speaker note for this part: two minutes on the glance and matrix, then "the per-active tables are in the appendix and in the workbook; you'll want them for MLR."

**Close** [5 min]

16. **Your call** — the claim board from slide 5 again, with two columns added: `Your read` (blank) and `Notes`. The ask, stated plainly: "Which three of these do you want to build on?" Speaker note is the most important in the deck: run the room row by row, get a reaction on each, land on at least three named directions before the meeting ends. Write them down by rank and Claim ID. After the meeting they go into the `Client picks` block of the Claim Set; that block is what Science Story clusters around. Without it the next stage stalls.
17. **What happens next** — the engine from here: your three picks → Claim Clusters → Science Story™ narratives → consumer testing → Science Studio™. One line on the testing decision. No timing commitments in writing.
18. **Closing** — "SCIENCE LEADS. CREATIVITY AMPLIFIES." + "Prepared by Let There Be for [Client] · [Month Year]".

**Appendix**

- A. **Per-active study tables** — the old backbone, unchanged in format: `Yr | Authors | Study & venue | Design | Finding | Key result | Relevance to [Brand]`, pivotal row highlighted, split across slides if >4–5 studies. Every study in the workbook appears here, including the ones that fed no claim.
- B. **Full study list** numbered A1, A2, … with resolving links, when the record set is large.
- C. **Regulatory notes** — class per SKU, permitted claim types, required disclaimers, anything marked `Flagged for legal`.

## The directions and their order

- They come from the Board in the Claim Set, ranked in Science Intelligence by the fixed rule (evidence strength → substantiation depth → product bridge → risk → ownability). Science set the order; this deck displays it. If the order looks wrong, the fix is in the Claim Set, not on the slide.
- Four to six chapters. Fewer than four and the deck feels thin; more than six and the hour runs out before the ask.
- The headline on slide 3 is the lead claim of chapter 1. Do not spend the best finding twice on two different claims.
- If the Board carries a `WHAT NOT TO SAY, AND WHY` row, it is the last chapter. Clients remember this; it is often the slide they quote back.

## Finding-direction coding (matches the evidence workbook)
POSITIVE PIVOTAL (dark green/teal dot, bold) · POSITIVE (teal dot) · MIXED (purple dot) · NULL (red dot — kept as a guardrail, never hidden) · NOTE (grey). Gradient bars for "studies by active" use the mitosis gradient.

## Rules
- Every study row and claim traces to a real citation from the evidence workbook. Never invent a study or statistic.
- No study appears in the main body without the claim it supports. A finding that supports no claim goes to the appendix or to the `EVIDENCE, NOT CLAIM LANGUAGE` slide.
- Every evidence slide in Part II ends with a payoff line carrying a Claim ID.
- Front matter is three slides. The engine, the model, and the opportunity are not restated up front; they get one line on slide 2 and one slide in the close.
- Show NULL/negative evidence and guardrails openly — it is what makes the claim set defensible and is a selling point of the LTB method.
- Claims are scoped to the product's regulatory class and carry the required disclaimer. Working (unapproved) language is always visibly labeled — use a `WORKING LANGUAGE · PENDING MLR` badge. Display each claim's ID exactly as the Claim Set assigns it; this deck never assigns or changes IDs.
- Claim lines, evidence sentences, guardrails, and consumer translations follow copy-craft.md. The evidence sentence on a card is written for a reader, not pasted from an abstract.
- Competitor claims on any slide are verbatim from the Competitive Intel tab with source and date. Never paraphrase a competitor or describe their evidence beyond what they publish.
- Speaker notes are a talk track, not a summary. Each carries the time budget, the two sentences to say, and (in Part II) the question to ask the client.

# Competitive Scan

The competitive market matters in every phase. We do not build claims other brands already make, we do not write in a voice another brand already owns, and we do not present Science Story directions a competitor could run under their own name. That only works if the scan is real: verbatim claims, from live sources, with URLs and capture dates. Claude's general sense of what a category "usually says" is not a scan and is never entered as one.

## When it runs

**Phase 1b**, after the regulatory frame and before the literature search. Knowing what the category already claims tells you which endpoints are commons and which are open before you spend search effort, and the competitor voice notes are needed later when claims are worded.

The scan is refreshed at Phase 6 if more than a few weeks have passed, and the Science Story skill re-reads it before writing narratives.

## Tools

- **Web search** (the chat's web search tool) is the default. If it is not enabled in the chat, say so and ask the user to turn it on; the scan cannot be done from memory.
- **Claude in Chrome**, when connected, for pages that search cannot read (retailer PDPs behind scripts, pack-shot galleries).
- **Client-supplied material** (competitor label copy, a category audit, a shopper deck) is entered alongside, with Source = `Client-supplied`.
- Elicit does not return label claims; do not use it for this.

If none of these are available, every claim's Market position is `Not scanned`, the deck displays it as such, and the ranking ignores the field. Never fill the gap from recall.

## Step 1 — Fix the competitor set

Start with the competitors the client named in the brief or kickoff. If none, search the category (`[indication] [format] brands`, top retailer category pages, "best [category]" round-ups from the past year) and propose five to eight. Include the category leader, the two or three closest positioned to the client, one science-forward brand, and one value or private-label brand. Confirm the set with the user before scanning; it shapes every ownability call downstream.

## Step 2 — Capture, per competitor and product

Visit, in order, until you have the front-of-pack and the primary benefit claims:

1. Brand site product detail page.
2. One major retailer PDP (Amazon, Walmart, Boots, Chemist Warehouse — whichever fits the market). Retailer copy often carries claims the brand site softened.
3. Pack front, from a product image, described in words.
4. Any HCP or "science" page the brand runs.
5. Substantiation the brand cites publicly (a "clinically shown" footnote, a study link).

Record, per competitor product, in the Competitive Intel tab:

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Competitor / Parent | |
| Product, SKU, format | |
| Regulatory class | as best inferred from claim type and labelling |
| Key actives and doses | from the label |

Also search for **rulings and challenges** in the category over the last five years: NAD/BBB National Programs (US), FTC actions, ASA rulings (UK), TGA (AU). A claim a competitor was made to withdraw is a guardrail for us. Log these on the same tab, Source = `Ruling`, with the claim, the outcome, and the URL.

## Step 3 — Build the Category Language Map

On the Competitive Intel tab (and mirrored on Overview), three short lists:

- **The commons** — endpoints and phrases that two or more competitors already claim. These are not opportunities as stated; they are opportunities only if our evidence lets us say them with a stronger qualifier, a specific number, or a mechanism no one else names.
- **The white space** — endpoints where our evidence is at least Moderate and no competitor claims it, or claims it only unqualified.
- **The category voice** — the words, sentence shapes, and taglines competitors share, plus any single-brand phrases that are effectively owned. This becomes the *avoid* list in copy-craft §11 for this project.

Each entry carries the competitor names and row references that support it.

## What the scan feeds

| Downstream field | Filled from |
|---|---|
| Claim Set › Market position | `Open` (no competitor claims the endpoint) · `Contested` (one or two do; name them) · `Commons` (category-standard) · `Not scanned` |
| Claim Set › Voice collision | words or phrasing in our line that echo a competitor's; `None` if clean |
| Board › Why ownable | names the competitors that do not say it, from the scan, not from recall |
| Deck › What the category says today | the Category Language Map, one slide |
| Deck › The differentiator (edge slide) | our line beside the competitor's verbatim line, with source |
| Science Story › Swap test | every concept is checked against the competitor set |
| copy-craft §11 project avoid list | the category voice |

## Rules

- Verbatim or nothing. A competitor claim that cannot be quoted with a URL and a date is not in the tab.
- Do not characterise a competitor's substantiation beyond what they publish. "Cites no study" is a finding; "has no evidence" is a guess.
- The scan is not a claim source. Nothing a competitor says becomes a claim of ours without its own substantiation in the workbook.
- Re-check before launch. Competitor copy changes; the tab carries its capture dates so a stale scan is visible.

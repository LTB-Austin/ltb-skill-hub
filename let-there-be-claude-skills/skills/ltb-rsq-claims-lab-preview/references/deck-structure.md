# Claims Lab Preview — the deck

This file mirrors the **current Claims Lab design template** in Claude Design (the Banana Boat Mineral build is the reference). The template has **15 screens**. Each screen below lists its template **slots** in the order they appear on the slide, so the finished brief can be pasted into Design field by field without rearranging anything.

Each screen is marked:

- **BOILERPLATE** — fixed copy. Reproduce exactly as written here. Only `{{ deckDate }}` changes, and Design fills that.
- **SEMI** — fixed frame and labels, variable content inside.
- **VARIABLE** — written for this product.

**Structure is fixed at three territories.** The template carries three product-context cards, three territory cards, three spotlight slides and three key-finding cards. Do not build two or four.

**Footer.** Every screen from 06 to 14 carries the footer `Preview · directional · findings not validated · sources masked until the full Lit Review™`. Screen 12 adds a second line. The template renders the footer and the page number; you do not need to write them into each screen, but do not change the wording.

**Speaker notes.** Every screen has one. It is a talk track for a presenter walking a room of regulatory, scientific and quality reviewers through it live. Three to five plain sentences. Speaker notes are where the limitations live now (see screens 08–10).

**Emphasis.** The template uses exactly one gradient-tinted card on screens 06, 07, 11 and 13. The brief marks which card gets it with `(emphasized)`. Mint and purple highlights appear only in spotlight tables.

**Length.** The word counts below come from the reference build. Staying inside them is what keeps the layout from breaking in Design. Lines marked *one line* sit on a single line with no wrap in the template.

## 07 Territories — VARIABLE

- **heading:** The territories we explored. *(fixed; no subhead)*

## 08–10 Spotlight studies — VARIABLE (one screen per territory)

- **eyebrow:** Science Intelligence™ logo | Lit Review™ logo | Spotlight studies *(fixed)*
- **legend:** mint `Supports the territory` · purple `Adds context` *(fixed)*
- **heading:** the territory name from screen 07, with a period.
- **table, exactly four rows.** Columns `Yr` · `Study` · `Key finding` · `Design`:
  - **Yr:** publication year. *Blurred.*
  - **Study:** two lines, both *blurred*. Line 1 `Author et al.` (or `Author & Author` for two authors). Line 2 `Journal abbreviation · PMID nnnnnnnn`, adding ` · NCTnnnnnnnn` when a trial registration exists.
  - **Key finding:** built in up to three parts:
    1. **lead-in (optional, plain):** the setup, 3–8 words, usually the population or n. "Of 65 top-rated sunscreens online,"
    2. **mint highlight:** the result, 6–16 words, with the number. "26 missed at least one dermatology basic: broad spectrum, SPF 30+, water resistant."
    3. **purple highlight (optional):** one sentence of context that a reviewer would raise, such as a competing ingredient that also performs, or a limit on who the result applies to. Use it on one or two rows per slide, not all four.
  - **Design:** 2–8 words: study type plus the first thing a reviewer checks. "Randomized; 5 mineral, 1 organic sunscreen." "In vitro, excised human epidermis." "Prospective multicenter photopatch study."
- Order rows so they build the argument: mechanism or strongest design first, the number the claim will use last.
- There is **no evidence note on the slide** in this template. Put the limitation of the set in the speaker note instead: in vitro only, small panel, self-report, dose above label, population that doesn't match the buyer.
- **speaker note:** Walk the rows in one sentence each (group them if two rows make the same point). Say what the purple line adds. Name the limitation of the set. Say which row's number the claims will use.

## 12 The claims — VARIABLE

- **heading:** `Four claims emerge.` (or `Three claims emerge.` if the evidence only carries three)
- **one card per claim**, `Claim 01`–`Claim 04`:
  - **claim line:** in quotes, one to three short sentences, each as short as it can be (copy-craft.md §1 targets ≤12 words a sentence). Lead with the number or the finding, then let the product arrive in its **own** sentence, so a category statistic never reads as a product result. "White tint is 3x worse on darker skin. Banana Boat® Mineral is formulated to blend in across skin tones."
  - **support:** one sentence, 10–25 words, plain, readable with citations blurred. It states the evidence the line rests on.
- No claim-type footer on the cards. Claim type, fit, risk and guardrail go on the presenter sheet.
- **second footer line:** `Illustrative and directional. Subject to MLR and legal review.`
- **speaker note:** One sentence per claim on the role it plays (the category gap, the population, the behavior, the formula). Say which claims own the open lane.

  2. What the problem costs them.

- **heatmap:** the headline again, then five short lines (≤12 words each) condensed from the narrative, each tagged `Strongly liked`, `Liked` or `Disliked`. Usually the problem statement is the one `Disliked` line; the product answer and its consequence are `Strongly liked`; proof and formula lines are `Liked`.
- **legend:** Strongly liked · Liked · Disliked *(fixed)*
- **speaker note:** This is what the next phase looks like. We take the claims you just saw and build them into one story. Then we test it with 200 to 300 real consumers for purchase intent, uniqueness and believability, plus a line-by-line heatmap of what they liked and didn't. Say plainly: these numbers are illustrative, not [Brand] data.

## Source masking

The room must see the science is real without being able to go read it. Blur **identifiers**, never substance. The template already applies the blur to the `Yr` and `Study` cells; if anything else carries an author, year, journal or identifier, it gets `filter: blur(4.5px); user-select: none` on that span only.

**Blurred:** publication years, author names, journal names, every PMID, DOI and NCT, and any author-year citation anywhere on a slide.

**Never blurred:** findings, n, designs, themes, claim lines, the story, and everything on boilerplate slides.

Because identifiers are blurred, every finding sentence must stand on its own. Never write "as Cole et al. showed."

The presenter sheet carries every identifier in full, so questions can be answered in the room.

## Rules

- 15 screens, in this order, labels as written. Three territories, four studies per spotlight, three key-finding cards, three or four claims, one worked example.
- Boilerplate reproduced exactly.
- The worked example is labeled illustrative on the slide and in the speaker note. No other slide carries scores.
- No prices anywhere.

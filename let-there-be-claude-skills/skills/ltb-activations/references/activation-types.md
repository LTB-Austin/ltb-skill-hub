# Activation types — spec for each

The standard client menu. Clients pick up to five. Each spec below is what "done" looks like.

---

## 15s video ad script

Hook-first short-form. The cutdown of the 30s, not a different idea.

- **Target 30–40 words of VO.** State the actual count on the screen.
- Three beats: the fact that sets up the need → the product line → the theme line over the pack shot.
- One efficacy claim only. Pick the one that scored highest on purchase impact.
- Two-column table: VISUAL | VO. Claim IDs listed under the table.
- Supers line: which disclaimers appear and when they come on screen.

## 30s education video script

The full narrative version of the Science Story.

- **Target 75–90 words of VO.** State the count.
- Beats: everyday setup → the mechanism in plain language → the shift or problem → the product → the benefit in use → theme line → pack shot.
- Two or three efficacy claims, each verbatim, each with its disclaimer.
- Visual column carries intent, not art direction. One sentence per beat, concrete and shootable.
- Recurring visual device is welcome (Qunol used two gauges labelled DEMAND and SUPPLY) as long as it doesn't visualize something the claim doesn't support.

## 60s HCP video script

Clinician-facing. Same science, different register.

- Named studies, designs, sample sizes, measured endpoints. Where the consumer script says "more than half of people," this says the trial, the n, and the endpoint.
- Mechanism stated properly, not simplified away.
- Still claim-disciplined: HCP audience does not unlock unapproved language.
- Separate disclaimer treatment — check whether the client's HCP materials carry different footnotes.

## eComm / DTC product page

The biggest build, and the one most often repeated per SKU. Five parts:

1. **Product title** — CURRENT vs PROPOSED, with a **Why** line. Usually the tail swaps a commodity phrase every competitor uses for approved claim language, and matches pack wording.
2. **Bullets ("About this item")** — numbered, each with a bolded label in caps and the claim beneath it, claim IDs in the margin. Mark which bullets are in-market and stay unchanged.
3. **Product description** — CURRENT vs PROPOSED.
4. **Image stack** — every carousel image annotated KEEP / CHANGE HEADLINE / CHANGE ONE ROW / REPLACE / ADD, with the reason and the claim ID. Call out where a new image is needed and what it shows.
5. **A+ content** — same module-by-module annotation, numbered against the captured screenshot.

If the brand runs an Amazon Brand Store, treat it as a sixth part: hero, story band, product grid, format bands, footer. A new story band between hero and grid is usually the single highest-value recommendation.

### When a module is suppressed rather than outdated

CURRENT vs PROPOSED assumes the brand's copy is rendering and merely says the wrong thing. Sometimes it isn't rendering at all — a retailer replaces brand bullets with its own auto-generated panels, an A+ module fails to load, a field exists in the catalog feed but never reaches the page. Check for this before writing: if the live page shows no brand bullets, look at whether the same copy appears elsewhere (a Brand Store tile, the mobile layout, a sibling SKU's listing) before concluding the brand never wrote any.

The two cases need different handling, and conflating them puts a false claim in front of the client:

- **Outdated** — the copy is live and wrong. CURRENT vs PROPOSED as normal.
- **Suppressed** — the copy exists but the page isn't showing it. Write the PROPOSED block as if displayed, since the feed still drives search indexing, Brand Store tiles and mobile layouts. Put a one-line note at the top of the screen saying what is suppressed and where the copy does still surface. Flag the suppression itself as a platform issue for the client's retail team — it is not a copy problem and we cannot fix it with better words.
- **Absent** — the field is genuinely empty. Say so, and label the screen a build rather than a rewrite.

Never leave a CURRENT column blank without saying which of the three it is. "No current copy" reads as "the brand wrote nothing," which may be untrue and is the kind of thing a client corrects you on in the room.

## MOA description

Short written mechanism of action.

- One tight paragraph plus optional three-step breakdown.
- Plain language first, precise term second where it carries meaning.
- States what the active does, never what the disease does.
- This is the piece creative and animation teams work from, so it must be visualizable — describe the mechanism as a sequence of things that happen, in order.

## Influencer copy brief

Simple science language that gives creators accurate talking points.

- **Say this** — the approved lines, verbatim, with disclaimers attached.
- **Don't say this** — the restricted phrases, with a one-line reason each. Pull from the project's guardrails (e.g. "stronger," "nothing faster," "recovery," "cure").
- **The one fact to lead with** — the opening of the Science Story.
- **Required disclosure** — ad disclosure plus any product-specific footnote.
- Keep it one page. Creators do not read decks.

## FAQ / myth-busting

5–7 consumer-facing Q&As drawn from the Science Story.

- Questions in the audience's own words, not the brand's.
- Answers lead with the direct answer, then the substantiation.
- Each answer tagged with its claim ID.
- Good place to retire a misconception the lit review surfaced.

## "Other" on the brief

Clients sometimes write their own. Treat it the same way: identify the real artifact, spec it, write it current-vs-proposed if it already exists, claim-ID everything.

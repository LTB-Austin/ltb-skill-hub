# Deck structure

The proven order. Adapt the content, keep the spine.

## Section 0 — Context (only if something changed)

Include only when the competitive or market situation moved since the Science Story was approved.

- **What happened** — the competitor launch, the category shift. Show the evidence, labelled "shown for reference."
- **What it means for us** — what we're dropping, what we're opening on instead, what stays ours. A two-column comparison works: THEIR ARGUMENT vs NOT IN THEIR ARGUMENT, with our claim IDs on the second column.
- Close the section with one line: the revised direction, and the reassurance that every efficacy line stays an approved claim, verbatim.

Skip this section entirely if nothing changed. Don't manufacture a pivot.

## Section 1 — The story

- **Direction screen** — the theme line, big, with the one-paragraph story beneath it and the disclaimer.
- **Narrative screen** — the story in full on the left, a CLAIMS USED table on the right listing every ID and its verbatim text. This screen is what proves the story is made of approved parts.
- **The approved claims** — the full list, verbatim, numbered, with the ID legend (what AC/CX/IM/POS or the project's equivalent means).
- **MLR guidance** — "This Science Story is a foundation, not a live asset." Where the story gets used, how each line type is handled, and the visual guardrail. One screen, and it's the screen that keeps the client's legal team comfortable.

## Section 2 — Activations

- **Section opener** — name the activations in this deck and state plainly: illustrations for review, not deliverables going live.
- Then each activation in turn, per the spec in `activation-types.md`.
- Group by activation, then by SKU — not the other way round. The client reviews "all the 15s scripts" together.

## Section 3 — Next step

One screen. The client confirms and approves the direction, the claims cluster and the activations. Add the footnote block: the disclaimer, the ID legend, and the note that IDs are working IDs until a formal Claim Set assigns them.

## Layout intent, not styling

Everything above is content plus layout intent for Claude Design. Describe the shape — "two-column table," "four annotated tiles beside the captured screenshot," "claims table right rail" — and let Design do the rest. Don't spend effort making it look finished.

## Art direction — specify it, don't leave it to Design

A content brief that says "place captures here" leaves Design guessing, and guessing puts an image on a screen that is pure type. Give every screen an explicit **ART** line, naming either `Type-only` or the exact asset filenames.

Default to type-only. In a working deck most screens are: the story, the claim tables, the MLR guidance, the scripts, and every current-vs-proposed copy screen. A product title is text. A bullet list is text. Image-led screens are the ones where a live page is being annotated, and they are the minority — roughly a third.

Three rules worth stating in the brief itself, because they are what separates a deck that looks considered from one that looks assembled:

- **Product images sit at native aspect ratio**, uncropped, unshadowed, in a numbered strip. Never in rounded cards that clip the pack.
- **A+ modules are wide** — around 1464px — and want full-bleed or near it. Shrinking one into a quarter-screen tile makes its type illegible, which is the single most common way these decks go wrong.
- **One image per screen by default.** Annotation grids are the deliberate exception.

Alternating type-only and image-led screens is also what gives the deck its rhythm. Several image screens in a row read as a screenshot dump.

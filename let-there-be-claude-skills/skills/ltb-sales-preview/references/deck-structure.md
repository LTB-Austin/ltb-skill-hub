# Sales Preview — the deck (13 screens)

**Template:** the One A Day® Teen Multi Gummy For Her build (November 2026), https://claude.ai/artifact/AtDJ3XjuJZhkDRPna9utYV. Every screen below maps to one of its slides (slide id in brackets). Match its numbers, layouts, lengths and tone every time.

The template is a real client deck. Every word, number, claim, quote, product name and image of One A Day® content on it is a placeholder. Nothing from it carries over.

**Types:**
- **BOILERPLATE:** keep the template's copy. List the screen by label only.
- **SEMI:** a fixed frame with some product-specific text.
- **VARIABLE:** written for this product.

**On every screen**
- **Headlines** sit on one line (`white-space:nowrap`). Shorten the words, never the type.
- **Footer:** screens 03–12 carry `Preview · no brand input · findings not validated.` and the LTB wordmark. Screen 03 instead carries `Prepared by Let There Be · [Month Year]`.
- **Speaker notes:** 3–6 plain sentences, written as a talk track for presenting live to a brand team that may include regulatory or medical reviewers. Notes carry the reasoning the slide leaves out.
- **No boxed tiles for content.** The template uses open grids, rules and one gradient band per screen at most. Keep it that way.

## 03 Product introduction — VARIABLE [`s03-cover`]
- **eyebrow:** `Product introduction` *(fixed)*.
- **title:** `Brand® — Product name`, on one line, ≤ 42 characters, with the ® or ™ as the brand uses it.
- **four label rows** (the labels are fixed; the values are one line each where possible):
  - `For`: who it's labeled for ("Teen girls, 12 and up").
  - `Format`: the dose form and daily serving ("2 chewable gummies a day").
  - `In each serving`: the key actives with doses for the ones the deck will use, then the rest by name, ≤ 110 characters.
  - `Category`: the regulatory class and the brand owner ("US dietary supplement · Bayer").
- **packshot:** the exact SKU on white. Upload the image if you have it. Otherwise leave the slot empty with alt text naming the SKU.
- **speaker note:** what the product is, its class and actives, and the question the deck answers, phrased the way the brand team would ask it. Then: "Everything here came from a compressed pass at the public science and your public claims only. No brand documents, no conversations with your team. Every finding is directional."

## 04 The Claims You Make Today — VARIABLE [`p03-current-claims`]
- **eyebrow:** `Current claims · public sources only` *(fixed)*. **Heading:** `The Claims You Make Today` *(fixed)*.
- **two columns:**
  - **Left** (gradient band): the client product's name, then 4 of its claims, verbatim and in curly quotes, ≤ 95 characters each. Lead with the front-of-pack line.
  - **Right** (plain grey): `[Competitor product] (A top competitor)`, then 4 of its claims, verbatim, ≤ 95 characters each.

  Pair the claims row by row on the same subject where possible (immune against immune). Keep each brand's own capitalization, punctuation and ellipses.
- **source lines**, one under each column: where each brand's claims were read, and the date ("Front of pack · oneaday.com product page, read Nov 3, 2026").
- **What we see:** three short observations, ≤ 75 characters each:
  1. What both brands claim.
  2. What the competitor owns that the client doesn't, or the reverse.
  3. **Bold:** what neither says, ending `That's the open lane.`
- **speaker note:** where each set of claims came from (pack, product page, FAQ), why this competitor (same buyer, format and channel), what each owns, and the gap the territory will fill.

## 05 Our Preview Scan — VARIABLE [`p04-review-scope`]
- **eyebrow:** Science Intelligence™ mark | `Preview literature review` *(fixed)*. **Heading:** `Our Preview Scan` *(fixed)*.
- **funnel**, four numbers joined by arrows. Every number is the real count from this run:
  1. `[n]` searches run across the research literature and trial registry
  2. `[n]` papers pulled and screened
  3. `[n]` shortlisted on design, size and fit to [the product's audience]
  4. (gradient) `[n]` studies behind every claim in this preview
- **left column:** `Three areas searched`, then three bullets of ≤ 35 characters each, the search areas in plain words.
- **right column:** `What made the cut · published [first year]–[last year]`, then 3–4 rows, each a count and a design ("3 · randomized, placebo-controlled trials in [population]", "2 · meta-analyses, one pooling 10,933 people", "2 · US national nutrition surveys"). The counts add up to the last funnel number.
- **speaker note:** read the funnel as a sentence. Then add: "A full engagement runs the same process across hundreds of papers, in full text."

## 06 Key Themes from Our Preview Search — VARIABLE [`p05-key-themes`]
- **heading:** `Key Themes from Our Preview Search` *(fixed)*.
- **four themes** in a 2×2 open grid. Each theme has:
  - a **title** (h3, one line, ≤ 38 characters), stated as a finding ("The teen years build the skeleton");
  - a **finding** in 1–2 sentences, ≤ 150 characters, carrying its number;
  - **`In the [format]:`** (e.g. `In the gummy:`, `In the tablet:`), ≤ 70 characters, saying how the product's label matches the finding: the dose against the doses tested, which nutrients it carries and which it doesn't.
- At least one theme should show a limit or a gap (a nutrient the product lacks, or an endpoint that's disease language). Be honest here; it's what makes the rest believable.
- **speaker note:** one or two sentences per theme. Name any theme that must stay background and never become a claim, and say why.

## 07 Hero claim — VARIABLE [`s06-t1-the-claim`]
- **pill:** `Hero claim` *(fixed)*.
- **heading:** the territory name, 2–4 words in Title Case, ≤ 30 characters ("The Building Years"). The same heading appears on screens 08 and 09.
- **gradient band:** the hero claim in curly quotes, ≤ 115 characters. The category number goes in the first sentence. The product's role, or the brand's existing claim, goes in a second sentence.
- **Speaks to:** who the line is for, ≤ 60 characters.
- **Why this matters to [Brand]:** ≤ 200 characters. Quote the brand's current claim verbatim where one exists, then say what the science adds.
- **Substantiation:** blurred short citations (`Author year · Author year`).
- **speaker note:** where the number comes from (study type, size, population), and what each sentence of the claim does. Then: "The category number and the product never share a sentence."

## 08 Other ways to say it — VARIABLE [`s07-t1-supporting-claims`]
- **pill:** `Other ways to say it` *(fixed)*. **Heading:** the territory name.
- **four lines** in a 2×2 open grid, in curly quotes, ≤ 90 characters each, in this order:
  1. **The product-fit line:** why this product, for this audience, now ("Girls build bone fastest at about 12. One A Day® Teen For Her is made for 12 and up.").
  2. **The honest short version:** the claim scoped to what the product actually carries.
  3. **The brand-voice tagline:** no facts, so no substantiation line.
  4. **The bolder line:** the one the consumer would repeat.
- **Substantiation** under every line that carries a fact: blurred short citations, or `Supplement Facts` where the line rests on the label.
- **speaker note:** one sentence per line on what it does differently.

## 09 The evidence — VARIABLE [`s08-t1-the-evidence`]
- **pill:** `The evidence` *(fixed)*. **Heading:** the territory name.
- **left (hero number):**
  - eyebrow: study type and setting, ≤ 40 characters ("Long-term study · 5 US centers");
  - the hero number, ≤ 7 characters (a range is fine: "33–36%");
  - its meaning, ≤ 95 characters, finishing the number's sentence;
  - `Source:` with `Author et al. · PMID nnnnnnnn`, blurred.
- **right (three study rows), separated by rules:**
  - each finding in one sentence, ≤ 115 characters, written as `[What was given]: [who, how long], [the result]`;
  - under it, the design line `Design · n=[n] ·` (left clear), then `Author year · PMID` (blurred).
  - Order the rows so they build: the core trial first, then the population nuance, then durability or follow-up.
- **The honest line / Where it could break:** a red-tinted band, ≤ 200 characters. It names the strongest counter-evidence or limit in plain words. It is required on every preview.
- **speaker note:**
  - walk the hero number, then the three studies;
  - then the honest line: "this is where we show we look for the backside risk before a reviewer does";
  - then what stays out entirely and why (disease language for the category);
  - then the dose comparison: the doses the trials used against what the label carries.

  Compliance logic lives in this note and on the presenter sheet. There is no separate compliance slide in the preview.

## 10 Science Story worked example — VARIABLE [`s10-science-story`]
- **eyebrow:** Science Story™ mark | `Worked example` | the tag `Illustrative — not [Brand] data`.
- **left:**
  - `Title`: the story name, 2–4 words, ≤ 24 characters, one line ("Your Building Season").
  - `Science Story`: four short paragraphs, written to the end consumer:
    1. **Setup** (positioning copy, no facts), ≤ 130 characters. The consumer's real life right now.
    2. **The science**, ≤ 200 characters. The hero claim and the product-fit line, reworded only for flow.
    3. **The product**, ≤ 200 characters. What it brings, in label terms, and who it's made for.
    4. **The close**, ≤ 130 characters. The bolder line, then the brand-voice tagline.
- **right:**
  - `What testing gives back · illustrative`, with three dials (Purchase intent, Uniqueness, Believability) as whole percentages between 65 and 85.
  - `Which words did the work`: one passage of ≤ 330 characters drawn from paragraphs 2–4, with 5–7 phrases colored green (strongly liked), orange (liked) or red (disliked). Include at least one red phrase: a word real testing would catch, such as a term that sounds clinical or like a school worksheet.
  - The legend line, as in the template.
- **speaker note:** how the claims group into one named direction. Say how many claims the story carries and that every factual sentence is one of them. Name the red word and why testing would catch it. Then: "The scores are illustrative, not [Brand] data."

## Source masking
- **Blurred:** author names, years in citations, journal names, and every PMID, DOI and NCT. Use the template's own blur style.
- **Never blurred:** findings, n, designs, populations, claim lines, the story, competitor claims, and the source lines on screen 04 (where claims were read is public and dated).
- Every finding must make sense with its citation blurred. Never write "as Smith showed."
- The presenter sheet carries every identifier in full.

## Rules
- 13 screens, in this order. One territory, one hero claim, four other ways to say it, three study rows, one honest line, one worked example.
- Every number on screens 05–10 traces to a study the run actually read, or to the run's own counts.
- The product never shares a sentence with a category statistic.
- Scores appear only on screen 10, labeled illustrative. No prices anywhere.

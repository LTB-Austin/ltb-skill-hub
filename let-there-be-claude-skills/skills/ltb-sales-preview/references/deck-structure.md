# Sales Preview — the deck (10 screens)

The preview shows a prospect **how our process works** on one product: one direction, run the way a full Science Intelligence™ engagement runs it. Screens come from two templates:

- **SP** = LTB Template — Sales Preview (Science Marketing Engine) · https://claude.ai/artifact/VEB4pUCzcLnEPc1EXy6Hjk
- **SI** = LTB Template — Science Intelligence (Lit Review + Claim Dev) · https://claude.ai/artifact/3eLTXv8dToy1DrofTGXhxk

Each screen is marked:

- **BOILERPLATE:** keep the template's copy. List the screen by label only.
- **SEMI:** a fixed frame and labels, with some product-specific text.
- **VARIABLE:** written for this product.

**Footer.** Screens 03–09 carry the SP footer: the page number plus `Preview · no brand input · findings not validated.` and the LTB wordmark at bottom right. The SI slides have no footer, so add the SP footer element to 04–06.

**Headlines** sit on one line. Shorten the words, never the type.

**Speaker notes.** Every screen gets 3–5 plain sentences, written as a talk track for presenting live to a brand team that may include regulatory or medical reviewers.

## 05 The direction — VARIABLE (merged from SI s08, s09, s10)

This is one slide that carries the hero claim, the supporting claims and the evidence. Build it from the SI slides' own elements. Keep their markup and styles, and lay them out as described here.

**Header row** (from s08): a black pill reading `The direction`, two progress dots (the first one active), `Direction 01`, and at the right the product's square marker with the product name.

**Heading** (h2, one line, about 54px): the direction name, 2–6 words, ≤ 40 characters. ("What's thickening your shake")

**Rule** (hr), then **two rows**:

**Row 1: two columns, about 1000px and 600px.**
- **Hero claim card** (s08's gradient card, scaled down):
  - eyebrow `Hero claim · [ID]` with its tier marker (Core / Push / Stretch);
  - the claim in curly quotes, ≤ 110 characters, at about 44px, bold;
  - `Speaks to`: who the line is for, ≤ 70 characters.
- **Evidence hero tile** (s10's gradient tile):
  - eyebrow = the evidence type and year, e.g. `RCT, double-blind · [BLUR]2024`;
  - the hero number, ≤ 8 characters, at about 66px;
  - its meaning in one sentence, ≤ 100 characters;
  - the source pill, blurred.

**Row 2: four equal columns.** Over columns 1–2 put the label `Supporting claims`, and over columns 3–4 put `The science`.
- **Supporting claim cards ×2** (s09's card):
  - a register tag (`Number-forward`, `Short`, `[Brand] voice`, `Names a competitor (legal)`, `Bolder voice · brand's call`), its tier marker and the claim ID;
  - the line in curly quotes, ≤ 90 characters.
- **Study cards ×2** (s10's "The science" card):
  - the finding in one sentence, ≤ 90 characters;
  - the design line, `Design · n · population`;
  - then `[BLUR]Journal year`.

**Footer:** the SP footer, plus `Illustrative and directional. Subject to MLR and legal review.` as a second footer line.

**speaker note:** Say why this direction, and why it matters to this brand (the "why it matters" content lives here, not on the slide). Explain the hero number in plain words. Explain what each supporting line does differently. Name the weakest point in the evidence before the room does.

**Rules for this slide**
- One hero claim and two supporting claims, no more.
- The hero number must appear in a study the preview actually read. If it only appears in an abstract, say so on screen 06.
- The product never shares a sentence with a category statistic. Lead with the finding, then let the product arrive in its own sentence.

## 06 Compliance logic — VARIABLE (SI s11)
- **header row:** the pill `Compliance logic`, the second progress dot active, `Direction 01`, and the product marker.
- **heading:** the same direction name as screen 05.
- **six tiles** in a 3×2 grid. Each tile has a bold title of ≤ 30 characters, then 1–2 sentences of ≤ 120 characters. Cover these, in this order:
  1. **Where the number comes from.** The design, n and population of the hero study.
  2. **Why it fits this product.** Dose, form and population against the label: matches, partial or gap.
  3. **The honest line.** The counter-evidence or limit, stated plainly.
  4. **Say what the study found.** The words the claim must keep, and the bigger words it must not use.
  5. **The category line.** What the regulatory class allows (structure/function, monograph, cosmetic) and where the claim sits inside it.
  6. **Before MLR.** What's still open: full text, a dose bridge, a label check.
- **risk line:** `Risk` followed by each claim ID with LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH (e.g. `CG1 medium · CG2 low · CG3 high`).
- **depth line:** `Depth` followed by the evidence basis (e.g. `Preview pass · full text for the hero study, abstracts for the rest`).
- **speaker note:** Walk the six tiles in one sentence each. Say which claim carries the most risk and why that's still worth showing. This is the page legal and medical reviewers will read first.

## 07 Science Story worked example — VARIABLE (SP s11)
- **tag:** `Illustrative — not [Brand] data`
- **card:**
  - `Direction 01`;
  - **story name**, 2–5 words, with no ™ unless the brand owns the mark;
  - **Science Story:** two paragraphs. The first gives the problem and the product's answer (≤ 200 characters). The second gives the supporting point (≤ 140 characters).
  - **Claim cluster · tested together:** three pills, each a short handle of ≤ 32 characters for the hero claim and the two supporting claims.
- **scores:** `And this is what testing gives back · illustrative scores`, with Purchase intent, Uniqueness and Believability as whole percentages between 65 and 85. They are never presented as brand data.
- **fixed line:** the template's "Every cluster is tested with consumers or HCPs…" sentence.
- **And which words did the work:**
  - two short phrases highlighted as strongly liked;
  - then one sentence from the story with 2–3 fragments highlighted as Strongly liked, Liked or Disliked;
  - and the legend.
- **speaker note:** This is what happens next. The hero claim and its supporting lines group into a named direction with a narrative, and then it gets tested with consumers or HCPs, whole and line by line. The scores are illustrative, not [Brand] data.

The story reuses claim language. It adds no new claims or numbers.

## Source masking

The prospect must be able to see the science is real without being able to go and read it. Blur **identifiers**, never substance. Use the template's existing blur style (`filter:blur(6.75px)`), and mark blurred spans `[BLUR]` in the content file.

- **Blurred:** publication years, author names, journal names, every PMID, DOI and NCT, and any author-year citation.
- **Never blurred:** findings, n, designs, populations, claim lines, the story, and the compliance tiles.

Every finding must make sense on its own with its citation blurred. Never write "as Smith et al. showed." The presenter sheet carries every identifier in full.

- Boilerplate screens are listed by label only and never rewritten.
- Scores appear only on screen 07, labeled illustrative. No prices anywhere.

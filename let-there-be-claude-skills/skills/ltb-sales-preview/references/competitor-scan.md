# Competitor scan and white space

Screen 04 sets the client's current claims beside one top competitor's. The comparison is how the deck finds its open lane, so do it before choosing territories.

## 1. Capture the client's claims
- **Sources, in order:** the front of pack, then the brand's product page, then the brand's FAQ or retailer listing. Use Claude in Chrome or web fetch, whichever is available.
- **Copy each claim verbatim,** with the brand's capitalization, punctuation, asterisks and ellipses. If a claim runs past 95 characters, use an ellipsis at a natural break, the way the template does ("…specially formulated to provide nutritional support with key nutrients for young women."). Never paraphrase.
- **Record** the source and the date you read it.
- **Take 4 claims for the slide.** Note any others (energy, immune, and so on) for the speaker note and the presenter sheet.

## 2. Pick the top competitor
Pick **one** competitor: the product a buyer would most likely compare this one against. Match on all of these:

- the same audience (the age, sex or life stage on the label);
- the same format (gummy, tablet, powder, spray);
- the same channels (mass, drug, Amazon);
- a comparable price tier.

If several fit, prefer the one with the most visible shelf or Amazon presence, and the most claims written for that audience.

Label it `(A top competitor)` on the slide. In the speaker note, say in one sentence why it was chosen.

If the presenter names a competitor, use that one.

## 3. Capture the competitor's claims
Use the same method: verbatim, 4 claims for the slide, with the source and read date. Where the strongest audience-specific line sits on an FAQ rather than the pack, use it, and say so in the speaker note.

## 4. Find the white space
Lay the two sets side by side and sort the claims into three groups:

- **Both claim:** a shared line (immune, energy, bone). Neither brand owns it.
- **One owns:** language only one brand uses (puberty, growth and development).
- **Neither says:** the thing the audience most needs explained that no one is saying. This is the open lane.

Write the three "What we see" lines from this sort. The third line, in bold, names the open lane and ends `That's the open lane.`

## How the white space steers the territories
- Score every candidate territory on whether it lands in **neither says**, or deepens a **both claim** with science the competitor doesn't have.
- Rank down any territory that would only echo what the competitor already owns.
- The territory you recommend in the shortlist should usually sit in the open lane. If the evidence is stronger elsewhere, say so in the shortlist.

## Rules
- Only public sources. No logged-in or paywalled pages.
- Read-only: don't add to cart, sign up or submit anything.
- Never present competitor claims as false or misleading. Screen 04 reports what each brand says, nothing more.
- Record every URL and read date on the presenter sheet.

# Ingredient and quality signals commonly used by supplement-scoring platforms

**This is a checklist of things to look for, not a list of findings.** Nothing here goes in the report unless you observed it on the platform (methodology, flag text, per-ingredient note) or in the captured population (tier output). Where the platform is silent on a signal below, that silence is itself worth a line in the Platform Inventory.

## Testing and transparency signals
- Third-party testing claims and whether a certificate of analysis is linked
- Certifications: NSF Certified for Sport, NSF/ANSI 173, USP Verified, Informed Choice / Informed Sport, NSF cGMP, Non-GMO Project, USDA Organic, Clean Label Project
- Manufacturing: cGMP statements, FDA-registered facility, country of manufacture
- Label transparency: proprietary blends (amounts hidden) vs. fully disclosed amounts; "other ingredients" completeness
- Company signals: brand age, recalls, FDA warning letters, lab-result publication

## Form factors (bioavailability and tolerability)
Platforms frequently distinguish forms of the same nutrient. Capture form on every active and watch for platform notes on:
- Vitamin B12: cyanocobalamin vs. methylcobalamin / adenosylcobalamin
- Folate: folic acid vs. L-methylfolate (5-MTHF)
- Vitamin D: ergocalciferol (D2) vs. cholecalciferol (D3)
- Vitamin K: K1 (phylloquinone) vs. K2 (MK-7, MK-4)
- Magnesium: oxide vs. citrate, glycinate/bisglycinate, malate, threonate
- Zinc: oxide vs. picolinate, citrate, bisglycinate
- Iron: ferrous sulfate / fumarate vs. bisglycinate
- Calcium: carbonate vs. citrate
- Vitamin E: dl-alpha (synthetic) vs. d-alpha / mixed tocopherols
- Chromium: chloride vs. picolinate; Selenium: selenite vs. selenomethionine / SelenoExcell
- Vitamin B6: pyridoxine HCl vs. P5P; Riboflavin vs. R5P
- Chelated / "TRAACS" / "Albion" minerals as a labeled form signal
- Dosage form: tablet vs. capsule vs. softgel vs. gummy vs. powder; gummies often carry sugar and lower nutrient loads

## Dose signals
- Dose relative to RDA/%DV and relative to doses used in trials; "token" doses in gummies
- Upper-limit concerns (e.g. high-dose vitamin A as retinol, iron in non-iron-need audiences, high B6)
- Per-ingredient "effective dose" notes on the platform

## Inactive ingredients frequently penalized
- Artificial colors: FD&C Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Red 40, Blue 1/2, "lakes"
- Titanium dioxide
- Talc
- BHT / BHA, TBHQ
- Hydrogenated oils, partially hydrogenated oils
- Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, aspartame, acesulfame-K); added sugars in gummies
- Carrageenan, polysorbate 80, sodium benzoate, potassium sorbate, propylene glycol
- Magnesium stearate and silicon dioxide (often tolerated; check whether the platform flags them)
- Maltodextrin, corn starch, modified food starch, gelatin (for vegan-positioning platforms), soy derivatives
- Polyethylene glycol, shellac, carnauba wax (coatings)

## Ingredient-level positives platforms often reward
- Active forms of B vitamins, D3, K2, chelated minerals
- Inclusion of nutrients mass-market multis omit (choline, K2, magnesium at meaningful doses, omega-3s, iodine)
- Clean "other ingredients" list (short, recognizable, no colors)
- Clinically dosed branded ingredients with trademark attribution
- Allergen-free, vegan, non-GMO statements where the platform scores them

## How to use this file
1. Scan the platform methodology and flag text for each signal above; log what the platform says, verbatim, in the Platform Inventory.
2. Make sure the capture template records form and every inactive so the tiering script can test these signals in the population.
3. When the tier output surfaces an ingredient not on this list, don't drop it; add it to the findings and to this file for the next run.

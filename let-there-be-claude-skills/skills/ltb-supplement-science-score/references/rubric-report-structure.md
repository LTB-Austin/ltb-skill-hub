# Findings document structure — Rubric Pass

Write as `<Client>_Rubric_Pass_Findings.md`. It drops into the Discovery Report as "The rubric read" plus its share of "Why the client scores as it does," "Guardrail implications," and "What we know, and don't." Plain voice. Every number cites the workbook tab it came from.

ALWAYS use this template:

# [Client] — Rubric Pass: [Platform(s)]
Capture window · population size · score range · methodology version/date

## 1. Approach
Three to six sentences: platforms read, how criteria were characterized, how the population was built (n, score coverage), how ingredients were tiered, how the scoring model was rebuilt. Name the script and the workbook. This is the "how we got to the answer" the client needs to trust the rest.

## 2. The rubric read — how [platform] scores the category
For each platform:
- The score, its scale, its components as published.
- Criterion by criterion: what is published · what is observed in the population · what is not disclosed.
- The rebuilt scoring model table (criterion · direction · relative weight · statement type · evidence).

## 3. What separates the top of the category from the rest
Top decile vs. bottom quartile on the four variables: ingredient patterns, dose ranges, form factors, testing and transparency signals. Numbers from the Ingredient Tiers and Dose Bands tabs. Name confounds.

## 4. Ingredient tiers
Three short tables: Helps · Mixed · Drags. Each row: ingredient (form) · support · platform explicitly flags? · agrees with platform? · one-line note. Full table lives in the workbook; here show the rows with strong or moderate support.

## 5. Why [Client] scores as it does
Per client SKU: score and percentile · the specific penalties, mapped to score components · what already holds up · closest competitor and the gap explanation. Then the portfolio view: recurring penalties (first levers), SKU-specific penalties, components the brand already wins.

## 6. Guardrail implications
Constraint statements ordered by portfolio reach and observed score association. Each: the constraint · the variable it belongs to · SKUs affected · evidence · statement type · caveat. State plainly that a better score is a possible result of acting on these, not a promise.

## 7. What we know, and don't
Undisclosed weighting · gated fields · population coverage gaps · confounds · platform changes observed during the pass · capture dates. Note that the report does not replace the client's own legal and regulatory review.

## 8. Method for re-run
Pointer to the Method Log tab and the capture protocol; the exact steps, tools, script version and inputs a stranger needs to repeat the pass. Note what to watch for: methodology page changes, score-scale changes, new sub-scores.

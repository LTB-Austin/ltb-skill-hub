# Stage 2 — Polished Science Story™ (post client direction + built test-ready)

Purpose: take the client's chosen direction and turn it into the polished, substantiated Science Story — and structure it so the narratives, headlines, and claims can go straight to the consumer-testing partner. The narrative leads; the claims and sources are the substantiation behind it.

## The mapping slides (when the client sent preferred claims or reacted to directions)
- **Your claims, mapped** — one row per client claim: their wording · concept it lands in · Claim ID · status (Carried / lightly tuned · Consolidated · Replaced · Held) · one-line reason. Every client claim appears; none is dropped silently.
- **What carried over** — per direction: FROM [direction] blocks for each element kept, plus NEW THIS ROUND for anything added, with one line on why.
- Client wording that is approved or in-market is shown verbatim and tagged IN-MARKET / APPROVED. Never silently edited.

## Reading the client feedback first
Extract before building: preferred cluster/direction · cross-cluster claim requests, by Claim ID ("bring CLM-07 from cluster 1") · topics to explore · tone/framing preferences · guardrails (anything flagged for MLR or to avoid). Write this back to the user as a short list and get a yes before building any slide (SKILL.md, "Feedback read-back"). Honor the preferred direction as the backbone; pull in claims from other clusters only if explicitly requested.

## Slide order
1. **Title** — Brand + "Science Story™, Claims & Narratives".
2. **Process overview** — engine in current site language.
3. **Challenge** — carried from Stage 1, lightly refined.
4. **A Note** — the "compass, not a script" framing (see standard language below).
5. **Science Story™ — Cover** — a brand-affirmative headline that resolves the challenge and names the brand; one-line subhead restating the promise in plain language. Lead with what the brand *is/does*.
6. **Science Story™ — Body** — the narrative (left; five beats per copy-craft.md §9; product named in full; ends on a callback to the title) + **Claims Used** table (right): Claim ID | Claim Name | Core Idea. Length is set by the medium and stated on the slide: 90–180 words for a deck or test stimulus, 75–90 for a 30-second script, 150–200 for a 60-second script. When several narratives go to test, each gets its own Body slide and they stay within about 20% of each other in length. If the narrative is longer than the test needs, the test version is a condensation of it, labeled as such, and both are kept.
7. **Activation Roadmap** *(optional)* — Activation | Science Story™ messaging to build from. Include only if activations are specified.
8. **MLR Readiness** — one slide, always included: the net-impression sentence for each headline and narrative; claims with substantiation depth other than `Full text verified`; derived claims awaiting scoring; counter-evidence the Claim Set records and how the copy accounts for it; safety carry-through items; anything flagged for legal.
9. **Polished Claims — definition** — "Claims are distilled, defensible statements…" with a POLISHED label.
10. **Claim slides (per claim):** Claim Body slide + Source Card slide(s).
11. **Test Plan** — the test-readiness section (see testing-readiness.md).
12. **Next steps** — 3 steps with dates.

## A Note slide (standard language)
1. **The text inside each claim** is not final consumer copy — think of it as a compass, not a script. Each claim is a scientific pillar of the Science Story.
2. **Claim Opportunities** are how those pillars show up in consumer-facing copy.
3. As we move into the final **Science Story** and **Activations** ([list deliverables in scope]), we shape every message around your MLR team's input and work with you to get items approved.

## Claim Body slide
- Multicolor claim mark left of the headline; headline large/bold with a divider beneath.
- Claim ID beside the headline. `Derived / unscored` tag if applicable.
- Full claim body (verbatim from the Claim Set).
- **Claim Opportunities:** numbered list of 3 consumer-facing claim lines (rules below). Each is a candidate test stimulus and will get a `C` code.
- LTBFavorite marker (bottom-left) if applicable — 2–3 per deck, selective.

## Source Card slide(s)
- Claim headline repeated as context header + divider.
- Two source cards per slide, each bordered: Study ID · Title · Author/Institution · Published (year) · Design and n · Direct Quote (verbatim, in quotes, taken from the Claim Set's Verbatim quotes field with its location; never paraphrased) · Applicability Note (2–3 sentences connecting evidence → the claim it enables, drawn from the Claim Set's element mapping and product bridge). >2 sources → more slides, 2 per slide.
- Show Primary sources first. If the Claim Set records Contradicting studies for this claim, add a short "What the evidence also shows" line naming them and how the wording accounts for them; this is what makes the card credible to MLR.

## Claim Opportunity writing rules
Consumer-facing (not clinical); name the product in ≥1 of the 3; grounded only in what the Claim Set's elements support (no new magnitude, population, timing, or qualifier); direct, specific, and plain (not hedged, not hyped); vary sentence structure across the 3; the most data-specific line comes last when a hard number exists, and that number is the one in the Claim Set with its denominator intact. Every line carries the disclaimer text from the Claim Set. Qualifiers follow the Claim Set's wording rules: "clinically shown" only where the claim's Primary study is an RCT on that endpoint.

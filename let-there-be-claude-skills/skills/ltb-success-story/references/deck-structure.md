# Success Story deck structure: seven screens

This is the fixed order. Each screen's footer reads `0n · Success Story · [Month Year]`, and the template fills it. The Lamisil build is quoted as the example for each field.

---

## 01 · Title

| Field | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | SUCCESS STORY | fixed |
| Brand | The brand name only ("Lamisil") | 1–2 words |
| Subline | The through-line: what was first or different, then "from claims to campaigns" or similar. "Lamisil's first mode-of-action story for US consumers, from claims to campaigns." | ≤ 90 characters, one sentence |
| Phase marks | Science Intelligence™ · Science Story™ · Science Studio™. Include only the phases that ran. | — |
| Asset | `[Insert hero packshot: product, transparent background]` | — |
| Speaker notes | What the project was, the product and indication, and the engagement window ("April to October 2026"), plus what the deck covers. | 1–2 sentences |

---

## 02 · The challenge

| Field | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | THE CHALLENGE | fixed |
| Headline | The strategic job, as an imperative ("Differentiate Lamisil on mechanism and speed.") | one line, ≤ 50 characters |
| The product | What it is, its active and its key fact ("Lamisil AT Cream and Spray is a terbinafine treatment for athlete's foot that works in one week.") | 1 sentence |
| The problem | What the consumer wants, what they don't understand, and what that costs them | 2 sentences |
| What [Brand] asked for | The client brief in their terms ("An eCommerce-focused campaign that raises product awareness, education, and drives sales.") | 1 sentence |
| Engine card | Heading "We deployed the Science Marketing Engine™". One row per phase that ran, each with its mark and one sentence on what it produced. Science Intelligence™: "A review of the [active] scientific evidence, and the claims it supports." Science Story™: "A new consumer narrative to guide all campaign content and storytelling." Science Studio™: name the assets ("An Ad-to-Education Sequence™: a 15-second ad and a 30-second education video.") | 1 sentence per row |
| Speaker notes | The problem in one breath ("Terbinafine kills the fungus; most azole creams slow it. Nobody on the shelf was explaining that.") | 2–3 sentences |

---

## 03 · Science Intelligence™

| Field | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Mark | Science Intelligence™ | — |
| Big number | The count of new approved claims ("12") | number |
| Number label | "New approved claims." | fixed shape |
| Approval line | "Approved through [approver] MLR and available for [market] consumer marketing." | 1 sentence |
| Insight headline | The core scientific contrast ("Terbinafine kills the fungus. Most creams only slow it.") | one line, ≤ 60 characters |
| Body 1 | What we did ("We investigated the scientific literature on [active] to strengthen existing claims and develop new ones.") | 1 sentence |
| Body 2 | Why it matters to the shopper ("Most shoppers can't tell antifungals apart. The new claims state what makes terbinafine different.") | 1–2 sentences |
| Claim grid label | "Lead claim in each territory" | fixed |
| Claim cards | Up to 6 cards. Each has a short territory label ("1 week vs 4 weeks", "Kills, doesn't slow") and the **approved claim verbatim** in quotation marks. Highlight one card: the claim with the clearest number. Use 4 or 6 cards, never 5, to keep the grid even. | label ≤ 25 characters, claim ≤ 160 characters |
| Speaker notes | How many claims, who approved them and for which market, and that the cards show the lead claim per territory. | 2–3 sentences |

**Fallback, Science Intelligence didn't run** (e.g. the client supplied the claims): drop the big number. Title the screen "The claims we built on" and show the approved claims used, with their source named.

---

## 04 · Science Story™

| Field | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Mark | Science Story™ | — |
| Headline | The story line ("Built to finish the fight.") | one line, ≤ 40 characters |
| Narrative beats | 4 short paragraphs in the order the consumer needs them, then a closing beat in bold: "[Brand]: [story line]." Each beat restates an approved claim in story order. It must not add meaning the claim doesn't carry. | each ≤ 140 characters |
| Comparison card | Up to 2 head-to-head bars, each with a heading ("Treatment length", "Recurrence at 12 weeks"), the brand's value (highlighted bar) and the comparator's value (grey bar). Use the same source study for both. | — |
| Source line | The study behind the numbers: design, comparator and citation ("Terbinafine 1 week vs clotrimazole 4 weeks, randomized double-blind trial (BMJ 1993).") | 1 line |
| Speaker notes | How the beats were ordered, and which two numbers carry the story. | 2–3 sentences |

Bar widths are proportional to the values, and the template sets them. Give the raw values only.

**Fallback, Science Story didn't run:** make this screen "The message", using the brand's approved consumer message and no comparison card.

---

## 05 · Science Studio™

| Field | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Marks | Science Studio™, plus the deliverable's sub-brand mark if it has one (Ad-to-Education Sequence™, MOA Moment™, Influencer Science Kit™) | — |
| Headline | What was made, in the order a shopper meets it ("One ad and one education video, in shopper order.") | one line |
| Asset tiles | 2 tiles (1–3 allowed), left to right in the order a shopper meets them, joined by an arrow. Each has a frame (`[Insert frame: …]`), a big length or type ("15s" / "ad", "30s" / "education"), a job headline ("Earns the click." / "Earns the belief.") and a placement line ("Paid media placements." / "On the product page.") | job ≤ 20 characters, placement ≤ 35 characters |
| Speaker notes | How the assets work in sequence, plus production facts: shoot location and month, and approval date ("Shot in Montreal in July, approved August 13.") | 2–4 sentences |

**Fallback, Studio didn't run:** drop this screen and renumber, or replace it with "Where the story went" if the client produced the content itself. The deck then has six screens.

---

## 06 · Impact & results

| Field | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | IMPACT & RESULTS | fixed |
| Headline | Where the work is live now ("Lamisil now has the Ad-to-Education Sequence™ running on e-commerce.") | ≤ 75 characters, one or two lines |
| Rollout line | Where else the claims and story are going, and through whom ("The new claims and Science Story™ are rolling out across product pages, the brand site and retailer sales decks through Emerge (The Emerson Group).") | 1–2 sentences |
| Results *(only if the client shared and cleared them)* | 1–3 figures, each with its timeframe and source | — |
| Client quotes | 1–2 quotes, verbatim, each with "Name, Title, Brand" | each ≤ 200 characters |
| Photo grid | 4 photos with short uppercase captions ("ON SET, MONTREAL", "SHOOT DAY, JULY", "CREW AT GOLDEN HOUR", "CLOSING CALL"), each `[Insert photo: …]` | caption ≤ 24 characters |
| Ratings strip *(only if a survey exists; `showRatings`)* | Six cells: Quality · Communication · Expertise · Value · Timeliness · Work again, each with the client's answer as given ("Very satisfied", "5 of 5", "Excellent", "Very likely") | as given |
| Speaker notes | What's live, who's rolling it out, the photo context, and where the quotes came from (e.g. "the closing call on September 18"). | 2–4 sentences |

# Science Intelligence Preview — slide structure

Delivered as **PDF**. Unbranded content draft only; Claude Design applies the brand system and exports.

The deck runs about 13 slides. Four of them are the same Science Marketing Engine graphic with a different phase highlighted, which is what frames the preview as a step in a larger process rather than a one-off report.

---

### 1. Cover
Let There Be. Product name. Month and year.

### 2. The Science Marketing Engine
The three phases: Science Intelligence, Science Story, Science Studio. Nothing highlighted yet. One line on what the engine does.

### 3. Preview intro
Product name and product image. State the disclaimer plainly:

> No brand input. No brief. No guidance. No client inputs. Everything here came from public literature.

That constraint is the pitch. It shows what an hour of the engine produces cold.

### 4. Engine — Science Intelligence highlighted
Same graphic, Science Intelligence lit. One line: this is where every engagement starts.

### 5. The Lit Review
One page. 8 to 10 studies relevant to the product and its actives. Table columns: year, author, study, design, finding.

**Sources delinked and blurred.** This slide is there to look like the real thing at a glance. The rep will not read it aloud. Do not spill onto a second page.

Carry the Lit Review mark so the stage is obvious.

### 6. The finding
The hero insight. One specific, quantified statement, in plain language, large.

Sources blurred. Add one line on why it matters commercially. This is the slide the rep actually speaks to, so it must stand on its own without the table.

### 7. Claim Dev intro
One slide. Claim Dev reads the literature and pulls out what is substantiated, regulatory-friendly, and strategically useful. Carry the Claim Dev mark.

### 8. The claims
One to three claims, all built on the hero insight. Consumer-facing language only, in quotes. Evidence blurred.

Per claim: the language, its tier (DTC-ready / DTC + HCP / HCP hero), and one line on why it is ownable.

Include a short category whitespace read: what nobody else in the category is saying.

Footer: Illustrative and directional. Subject to MLR and legal review.

### 9. Engine — Science Story highlighted

### 10. Example Science Story
A single slide holding the whole idea:

- **Creative hook** — the line the campaign would hang on.
- **Narrative** — three or four sentences.
- **Claims used** — the claims from slide 8.
- **Testing line** — we build several of these and test them with consumers on purchase intent, uniqueness, and believability, including highlight heat maps.

Design treatment: this slide should feel more premium than the rest. It is the one that sells the next phase. No price quoted.

### 11. Engine — Science Studio highlighted

### 12. Science Studio work
Boilerplate. Example stills from published LTB work. The rep supplies nothing. Rotate examples as the reel changes; brand selection does not matter much here.

### 13. Closing
Unlock your science potential. Contact details.

---

## Rules

- Every science or claims slide: **Preview · no brand input · findings not validated.**
- Every source on every slide is blurred or delinked.
- One hero insight. One to three claims. Do not expand.
- No "your product and category context" slide. It was cut for being text-heavy and unused.
- No testing price in the deck.

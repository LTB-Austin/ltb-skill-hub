# Science Marketing Engine Preview — the 12-slide template

This deck maps **1:1** to the Claude Design template. Produce exactly these twelve screens, in this order, with these labels. Do not add, merge, split, or reorder slides unless the rep explicitly asks. If you have more material than a slide holds, cut the material.

Each slide below is marked:

- **BOILERPLATE** — fixed copy. Reproduce as written. Only the date changes.
- **VARIABLE** — you write it for this product.
- **SEMI** — fixed frame and headings, variable content inside.

Every screen carries a `data-screen-label` in the numbered form shown, and a **speaker note** written for a rep who has not read the studies.

## 05 The evidence — VARIABLE
Heading: **What the literature already says.**

A table of **four studies**, the ones behind the hero finding. Columns: `Yr` · `Study` (author et al. + PMID) · `Key finding` · `Design` · `Signal`.

Write each key finding in one plain sentence a marketer can read aloud. Bold the phrase that carries the point. Where a study is the brand's own trial, say so in the row.

Below the table, one line: **"Also in the pass:"** naming two or three further sources with identifiers, including a registered trial (NCT) where one exists.

**Source masking applies here.** Year, author name, and PMID are blurred. The key finding, design, and signal stay legible. See Source masking below.

## 06 Hero finding — VARIABLE
Heading: **Hero finding**, then the finding as a short two-part statement, e.g. "Menthol doesn't just cool the skin. It changes the signal."

Three numbered points, `01` `02` `03`, each one idea:
- **01** — the mechanism, with the named receptor, pathway or protein.
- **02** — the measured human result, with the comparator.
- **03** — the second-order advantage the mechanism creates.

Then a **Why it matters** block: what the category currently says, and what the literature lets this brand say instead.

This is the slide the rep talks to. It must stand on its own without the table.

## 08 Three claims — VARIABLE
Heading: **Three claims the evidence already supports.**
Subhead: research-backed, scoped to [the product's regulatory class], and rooted in something a shopper or a pharmacist already asks for.

Exactly **three** claims. Each block:
- The claim in quotes, with a trailing `*`.

Claim language is consumer-facing. No tiers, no risk matrix, no ID codes on this slide.

## Source masking

The prospect must see that the science is real and specific, without being able to go read the papers themselves. So the deck blurs the **identifiers**, never the substance.

Applied in Design as `filter: blur(4.5px); user-select: none` on the masked span only.

**Blurred:**
- Publication years in the evidence table
- Author names (`Johar et al.`, `Castro & Dent`)
- Every `PMID` and `NCT` number, wherever it appears
- Author-year citations inside claim support lines (`Li 2022`, `Higashi 2010`)
- The descriptive source phrase in the "Also in the pass" line

**Never blurred:**
- Key findings and what the study showed
- Study design and the signal chip (Positive, Mixed, Null)
- Hero finding copy, territory copy, claim language, guardrails
- Narrative, headline, and everything on the boilerplate slides

The rule in one line: **a reader can understand every finding and cannot look up a single source.** Write the finding text so it stands on its own without the citation, because the citation is unreadable by design.

## Rules

- Twelve slides. Six are boilerplate (01, 02, 10, 11, 12 and the frame of 04). Reproduce them; do not rewrite them.
- One hero finding. Two secondary territories. Three claims. One story direction. These counts are fixed because the template's layouts are built for them.
- Real PMIDs, present but blurred. Findings legible, sources masked.
- Testing scores stay empty. No price anywhere.
- Slide 09 carries **Illustrative — not [Brand] data.**

# Research Alignment Session — the deck

Built from the Tylenol, Habitrol and Complete Commensal Probiotic sessions. The **core** runs 23–25 screens. **Optional modules** add a screen each where the project calls for it.

Each screen is marked:

- **BOILERPLATE** — reproduce as written. Only bracketed items change.
- **SEMI** — fixed frame and labels, variable content.
- **VARIABLE** — written for this project.

Screens that split across two slides (priorities, exclusions) carry `cont.` on the second.

---

## Core screens

### 01 Cover — VARIABLE
- Title: `Research Alignment Session` (product name above it if the product is the subject, e.g. `Complete Commensal Probiotic™`)
- `Prepared for` — the client team or category, e.g. `ADULT PAIN`, `NICOTINE REPLACEMENT THERAPY`, `DESIGNS FOR HEALTH BRAND, MEDICAL, LEGAL, REGULATORY`
- Date of the session.

### 02 Agenda — SEMI
Six to eight numbered items matching the sections actually in the deck. Standard set:
1. The challenge
2. What we have already absorbed
3. How Science Intelligence™ and Science Story™ run
4. Where we search, and where we do not
5. Three directions to pressure-test
6. What we need to settle today (or: Guardrails and scope boundaries)
7. Timeline

### 03 The challenge — VARIABLE
Heading: `The challenge`
Two or three short paragraphs: the market shift or business problem; what the client wants; what our job is inside it. Use the client's own name for the opportunity if they have one. End on the job: "Our job is to help build the evidence base underneath it." / "This project is about new claims, not new messaging."

### 04 Why evidence, not positioning — VARIABLE
- Eyebrow: `WHY EVIDENCE, NOT POSITIONING`
- Headline, two lines: `A [type of argument] needs published evidence.` / `Brand language cannot carry it.`
- Paragraph 1: what makes this argument hard to approve and valuable to own (it compares against a competitor or class; it speaks to people with a condition or dependence). End: "It has to rest on published studies that hold up under your review, and under anyone who questions it later."
- Paragraph 2: tie to something the client said or wrote (their plan names regulatory readiness; their standard is "clinically proven"; they have no claims matrix yet). End: "This work builds that [readiness / standard] in advance, rather than under deadline."

### 05 We have scanned the material you sent — VARIABLE
- Heading: `We have scanned the material you sent.`
- Label: `THE POPULATION` / `THE CATEGORY` / `THE CONSUMER`
- **Four stat tiles**: a figure and a one-line label each. All from the client's materials.
- Label: `THE PATTERN UNDERNEATH IT`
- **Two observations**, one or two sentences each: the insight hiding in their data (condition stacking, dual use, a self-reported gap against clinical prevalence).
- **Source line**: the documents the figures came from, with dates and n where given.

### 06 What we have already absorbed — VARIABLE
- Eyebrow: `WHAT WE HAVE ALREADY ABSORBED`
- Heading: `The [segments / users / verticals], by [scale / what they use]`
- A table from the client's data. Two or three columns, e.g. `VERTICAL | POPULATION` or `USER GROUP | SHARE | WHAT STANDS OUT`.
- A note for anything their research does not cover ("Pouch users are not read as a separate group in the research we have received."), and a source line.

### 07 Approved language — SEMI
- Eyebrow: `APPROVED LANGUAGE`
- Heading: `What [Brand]® can say today, and where it stops`
- Left: the approved claims, verbatim in quotes. If we do not have them: `NEEDED FROM YOU: Your current approved [Brand]® claims, by SKU.`
- Line: "These are strong claims, and they have been in market for years. There are two places where evidence can add to them." (adjust the first sentence to fit)
- Right: `There is room to say more.` Then the two places, each a bold lead line and one or two sentences. Typical pairs: the evidence can *explain* what the approved claim only asserts; and the client's own data shows a population or behavior the current set does not cover.
- Close: "Both are questions the literature can answer. That is where Science Intelligence™ starts."

### 08 The engine — BOILERPLATE
Three columns:
- **01 · THE PROOF** — Literature review and claim development. What the science actually supports, and what you can therefore say. → Lit Review™ · Claim Dev™
- **02 · THE PERSUASION** — Claims assembled into strategic directions, written into narratives, and tested with consumers. → Concept and claim testing with Persuadable Research
- **03 · THE PRODUCTION** — Not in this scope. A separate conversation, held after results.

If Science Studio™ is sold, replace the third column's text with what is in scope.

### 09 How we search — SEMI
Four blocks:
- **Sourcing** — "We search the peer-reviewed literature and the clinical trial registries directly, prioritizing meta-analyses, systematic reviews, and randomized controlled trials. We weight to the last 10 to 15 years and include older work where it is seminal." Then one scope sentence for this project: "Searches are scoped tightly to the indication." / "Scope is the evidence on [X, Y and Z]." / "Searches are scoped to the organism and the benefit territory rather than to the category." Add government and public-health sources where they matter.
- **Verification** — "Every PMID and DOI is opened to confirm it leads to the study cited. Every number we carry into a claim is checked against its source."
- **Screening** — the inclusion criteria for this project, e.g. "Human studies. Relevant indication. Population and dose relevant to the product. Peer-reviewed." Note any special handling ("In vitro and animal work is recorded, tagged as such, and never carried into human-facing language.").
- **Extraction** — "Every study that survives screening becomes a row in a structured evidence workbook: design, sample size, comparator, quantified result, direction of finding, mechanism, relevance to the product, safety signal, and a resolving PMID or DOI."

### 10 Our evidence standard — BOILERPLATE
- Eyebrow: `OUR EVIDENCE STANDARD`
- "Abstracts routinely lead with a secondary or subgroup result when the primary endpoint missed, omit confidence intervals, and are loose on dose, form, duration, and population."
- `THE RULE` — "A study is read in full text before a claim rests on it when either is true: it is pivotal to the claim, or it is the sole support for the claim."
- "In practice that is roughly three to ten studies in a project. Everything else stays abstract-based, and every row records which it was."
- "Every deliverable carries a one-line method statement: how many studies were screened, and how many supporting the recommended claims were verified against full text."

### 11 How a claim is built — SEMI
- Lead: "Every claim carries the study behind it, written for the audience it is meant to move and inside the register [the regulatory frame] allows." Frames: "the OTC Monograph allows for an internal analgesic"; "[Brand]'s approved OTC labeling allows"; "DSHEA allows for a dietary supplement."
- **Consumer** — "Marketing forward. Written to sell: short, distinctive, built on the unique multipliers the evidence gives you, with the required disclaimers carried alongside rather than written into the line."
- **HCP** (or **Practitioner**, or **Pharmacist**) — "Same energy, more mechanism. Scientific language and an evidentiary anchor, still written to catch a clinician's attention rather than read like a journal abstract."
- Mark priority if one register leads (`PRIORITY 1` / `PRIORITY 2`). If the second register's scope is unknown, add `A QUESTION FOR TODAY: Is the [HCP] register in scope for this work, or is it consumer only?`
- `SUBSTANTIATION` — "Every claim can be broken into its component parts and substantiated piece by piece against the sources behind it: evidence strength, regulatory risk, and a guardrail note on what the evidence does not support." Add the client's submission format if known ("It arrives in the format your team submits in: PDF, with supporting documents.").

### 12 Risk ratings — SEMI
- Heading: `Every claim arrives rated for evidence strength and regulatory risk`
- Table `RISK | WHAT IT MEANS`, with definitions set for the class:

| Class | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| OTC monograph | Two or more RCTs at dose, permitted language, disclaimer present | Single RCT or review, mechanism claim, boundary language | Comparative, disease-adjacent, or thinly supported |
| OTC with approved labeling | Two or more RCTs, language inside approved labeling, disclaimer present | Single RCT or review, mechanism claim, boundary language | Comparative against [named alternatives], outside the labeled indication, or thinly supported |
| Dietary supplement | Two or more human RCTs at or near dose, structure/function language, FDA disclaimer present | Single RCT or consistent reviews, mechanism claim, strain or dose mapping is partial | Comparative or first-in-category without a stated basis, disease-adjacent, or animal and in vitro only |

- Closing line, specific to the project: "On this challenge, the strongest claims are likely to be comparative ones. We will show them alongside your approved language, so you can see what the evidence adds."

### 13 Science Story™ — SEMI
- Heading: `This is where the claims you choose come to life.`
- "You shortlist the claims worth building on. We turn them into [three to five] strategic directions, each with its own narrative and the claims that hold it up."
- Three steps:
  - **You shortlist** — The claims you want to build the argument on.
  - **We build** — [Three to five] directions, so you see the range before committing.
  - **Three go to test** — Real consumers tell you which one moves them. (Add "The winner becomes your messaging platform and collection of claims." where that is the deliverable.)

### 14 Consumer test · worked example — SEMI
- Eyebrow: `CONSUMER TEST · WORKED EXAMPLE`
- Left, `DIRECTION [X] · STIMULUS SET`, using one of the three directions from screen 21:
  - `HEADLINE` — a sample headline for that direction.
  - `NARRATIVE` — two to four sentences, ending in a conversation prompt where appropriate ("Talk to your pharmacist about what fits you.").
  - `CLAIMS, TESTED INDIVIDUALLY` — "01 · Substantiated statement, rated for evidence strength and regulatory risk." / "02 · Substantiated statement, with its guardrail note attached."
- Right, `WHAT COMES BACK ON EACH DIRECTION`:
  - **Purchase intent** — The primary read. Shift against a control cell.
  - **Believability** — The one to watch on [this kind of argument].
  - **Message heatmap** — Line-by-line highlighting of what lands and what loses them.
  - **Diagnostics** — Uniqueness, relevance and clarity, scored per stimulus.
  - **Claim ranking** — Which individual claims carry the direction, and which do not.
- Footer: "Illustrative format. Claim copy stands in until the evidence is in hand."

### 15 Consumer test · who we test with — SEMI
- Eyebrow: `CONSUMER TEST · WHO WE TEST WITH`
- Heading: `The characteristics we want in the sample.`
- "Not a segmentation plan. Four things we would like the recruit to reflect, and your view on each in session."
- Four labeled blocks, one or two sentences each. Typical: **Age**, **Has a condition** / **Nicotine use**, **Current behavior** (uses the competitor), **Occasion** / **Intent**, **Readable groups**.
- Optional scope line: "What this test is: it reads the three Science Story™ directions. It is not a foundational study of [X]."

### 16 Deliverables — SEMI
- Eyebrow: `DELIVERABLES`, heading `What you end up with`
- Five blocks (adjust names to the SOW):
  - **The evidence workbook** — Every study, verified, with its result, direction, and identifier. Yours to keep and to hand to anyone who asks.
  - **The claim set** — Every claim at three expression levels, rated for evidence strength and regulatory risk, with guardrails attached.
  - **The Science Story™** — The winning direction, its narrative, and the claims cleared to support it.
  - **Testing results from Persuadable Research** — What real people in [the target group] found persuasive and believable, and what they did not.
  - **The handover document** — At the end of the project, all of it compiled into a single document.

### 17–18 Where we search first — VARIABLE
- Heading: `Where we propose to search first` (`cont.` on 18)
- Subhead: "Ranked on [population, opportunity value, and how much room the evidence gives us beyond what you already claim]. Proposals, not decisions."
- Four or five rows: **Priority N — [Territory]**, a size or rationale line (`80MM people · $806MM pool`; `Vape + cigarette, pouch + cigarette, and more than one product`), and two or three sentences on the lines of inquiry.
- Optional final row: **Exploratory — [Territory]**, `A scan rather than a priority`.
- Optional `A QUESTION FOR TODAY` or `TO BE DEFINED WITH [CLIENT]` where scope is genuinely open.

### 19–20 Where we propose not to spend the time — VARIABLE
- Heading: `Where we propose not to spend the time` (`cont.` on 20). Subhead: "Proposed. To confirm today." or "Stated so it is clear this is deliberate rather than overlooked."
- Four to six rows: the territory, its size if known, a tag (`Proposed out` / `Out of scope`), and the reason in one to three sentences.
- Optional closing: a note on what stays central despite thin client data, and a question for today.

### 21 Three directions to pressure-test — VARIABLE
- Eyebrow: `THREE DIRECTIONS TO PRESSURE-TEST` (or heading `Three Potential Directions Right off the Bat`)
- Subhead: "Not conclusions. Candidate shapes for an argument [against the alternatives], so we know what to watch for as we search."
- Three columns: `DIRECTION A` / `B` / `C`, each a short name and one paragraph that ends on its regulatory difficulty.
- Optional `THE QUESTION FOR TODAY` beneath.

### 22 What we need to settle today — VARIABLE
- Heading: `Questions we need answered today` or `What we need to settle today`
- Five to seven items. Either numbered questions with a one-line "why it matters," or bold labels (`Comparator.` `SKUs.` `Source threshold.` `Reviewers.` `Audience.` `Objective.`) with the question.

### 23 Guardrails — VARIABLE (include when the client has not yet given them)
- Heading: `Guardrails`
- Arrow list of what we need from them: restricted language and previously rejected claims; competitor-owned language; position on comparative claims that name [competitors]; portfolio constraints; conditions or populations off-limits; where the product's own labeling limits the argument.

### 24 Timeline — VARIABLE
- Eyebrow: `TIMELINE`. Headline anchored to the business moment ("Three months, from today to January quitting season." / "Two paths. The difference between them is entirely on your side of the calendar.")
- Table `STAGE | DATES` (or `STAGE | ACCELERATED | STANDARD`). Standard stages:
  1. Research alignment session
  2. Science Intelligence™ build (Lit Review™ + Claim Dev™)
  3. Science Intelligence™ presentation
  4. Claim priority shortlist returned
  5. Science Story™ build
  6. Science Story™ presentation
  7. Review round / first regulatory pass
  8. Consumer test in field
  9. Results and winning story
  10. Final review round
  11. Handover (and activation session, or messaging platform delivered)
- Side note, `ON [BUSINESS MOMENT]`: what the client has in hand and when; the client-side date that holds the plan together; the date to confirm today.

### 25 Closing — BOILERPLATE
`Thanks for trusting us with your science.` / `Science leads. Creativity amplifies.`

## Optional modules

Insert where noted. Use them when the project calls for it; say in the handback which you used and why.

### What this session is for — after the agenda
Use when Medical, Legal or Regulatory reviewers are in the room, or the client has a science team that will scrutinize method.
- "We have not opened the literature yet. That is deliberate. Before we do, we want your [medical] team to know how we work, and we want to be sure we are starting from the right materials and the right boundaries."
- `Three things to leave with:` 1. Agreement on the evidence standard we will hold. 2. Confirmation that we have the right source materials, and clarity on what is missing. 3. A working process that gets claims through your review without overwhelming it.

### What we have — after "What this session is for", or before screen 05
Use when the client sent a large or technical asset set.
- Heading `What we have`. "Materials received and held in the project. We want to confirm this is the complete and current set before we begin."
- Table `SOURCE | WHAT WE UNDERSTAND IT TO BE`, one row per asset, with dates. Where our reading of a document matters, say it and ask: "Our reading is that it maps the literature rather than functioning as a claims document. Please confirm."

### Your regulatory foundation is already built — after Approved language
Use when the client has an approved claims architecture.
- Heading: `Your regulatory foundation is already built. This does not start it over.`
- **Extend** — We search the wider literature for what the evidence supports that the approved set does not yet say.
- **Substantiate** — Every new claim arrives with the published sources behind it, broken into its component parts.
- **Grade** — Every claim arrives with evidence strength, regulatory risk, and a written note on what the evidence does not support.
- "The output is a file your reviewers can work from, and that [Client] can hand to anyone who challenges a claim later."

### Where the evidence lives — after How we search
Use when there is no published work on the exact product (a novel formula, a first-of-kind combination).
- Heading `Where the evidence lives`. One paragraph: there is no published literature on this exact product; the case gets built one level down.
- Three rows: **On the [ingredients]**, **On the mechanism**, **On the gap**.
- A note: "Strain-exact evidence is not the goal. The goal is a documented case for why this product exists and why it is different, with every piece of it traceable to published work."
- Practical notes on mapping (different strain, different dose unit) and what needs agreeing.

### Comparative and first-in-category claims — after Risk ratings
Use when the product's differentiators are largely comparative.
- Three pressures: **FTC** (competent and reliable scientific evidence, matched to the net impression a claim creates rather than its literal wording), **Competitor challenge**, **Retail review** (e.g. Amazon content standards at go-live).
- "Our position is not to avoid these claims. It is to build them so they hold. Every comparative claim we recommend arrives with the competitor set it was measured against, the basis of comparison stated explicitly, and the surveillance condition that keeps a first-and-only claim true over time."

### When to bring your reviewers in — before Timeline
Use when the client's MLR is in the room or review capacity is a known constraint.
- Two things arrive before anyone needs to review anything: **The workbook** and **The claim set** (directional).
- `OUR RECOMMENDATION` — "We do not recommend putting the full claim set through MLR. A long list of claims with nothing attached to them is hard to review and rarely produces a useful answer."
- **Reviewers come in at Science Story™** — By then the priority claims sit inside a narrative, so there is something concrete to react to.
- **Individual claims, as you want them** — If there are claims from this work you want on your claims list, we work with you to get what those specific claims need.
